    After the profiles

    Four projects that turn thirty biographies into student work. Run them in order or pick one.

    DOS VIDAS, UNA CONEXIÓN

    Two lives, one connection

    Choose two people who worked in different fields. Find one shared idea, such as access, identity or service. Use one fact about each person to explain the connection.

    Both ___ and ___ contributed by ___. Their work differs because ___.

    TU REPORTAJE

    Your research feature

    Choose one person and investigate one question. Use two sources. Record the author or organization, page title and link. Write a 100-word profile with a verified contribution and an explanation of its impact.

    Add a captioned illustration, a short Spanish summary and one question for readers.

    GALERÍA DEL AULA

    Classroom gallery

    Publish your profile as a magazine page. Display the pages. Read three classmates' profiles and leave a specific question or comment supported by what you read.

    Explain one connection between a featured person and an issue in your community.

    LO QUE APRENDIMOS

    Evidence of learning

    Which contribution changed your thinking? Name the person. Explain the contribution using two accurate details. Describe why it matters and identify a question you still want to investigate.

    Success criteria: accurate facts, clear explanation, source credits and readable presentation.

    Notes for teachers

    • Read one English biography, use the Spanish for comprehension support, and discuss the profile question. Spread the profiles across several lessons rather than one sitting.
    • Allow about five minutes for a comparison, a separate class period for research, and ten minutes for gallery discussion.
    • Offer bilingual notes and partner rehearsal before any student presents.
    • Assess content evidence separately from English grammar.
    • Every discussion question is an open inference, not a fact check. Ask students to point to the sentence in the biography that supports their answer.
    • The illustrations are AI-generated interpretations, not photographs. Say so when you display them, and use the linked source for verified facts.
